5. Limits of AI, OCR and medicine information

AI answers, summaries, translations, image descriptions and OCR transcriptions may be incomplete or wrong, including medicine names, strengths, schedules and report values. They support understanding and professional review; they are not a diagnosis, a new prescription or an instruction to start, stop or change treatment. Verify important information against the original document and a qualified clinician or pharmacist. Medicine availability, manufacturer details and prices may change. A medical image cannot be safely declared normal solely because an AI response sounds reassuring.

6. Mental-health and wellbeing features

AI wellbeing conversations and optional creative features, including a requested caricature, are not psychotherapy, psychiatric assessment or a promise that symptoms will improve. Use them only as supplementary support. You may decline an optional photo or creative feature. Seek qualified professional care when appropriate. The emergency limitations in section 4 apply equally to mental-health situations.

7. Consultations, consent and recording

Remote consultations can have clinical and technical limitations; a doctor may require an in-person examination or decline unsuitable remote care. Consultations may involve server-side audio recording and AI transcription for consultation notes, draft prescriptions and summaries, as explained in the Privacy Policy. Appropriate advance notice and any legally required specific consent are necessary; camera or microphone permission alone is not consent to recording. Do not record or disclose another participant’s consultation without lawful authority. AI-generated clinical drafts must be reviewed by the responsible clinician before issue.

A displayed doctor, time or chat proposal does not guarantee a completed booking. Complete any requested confirmation before assuming a chat-assisted action has taken effect. Account permissions determine which records and scheduling actions are available. Do not use chat instructions to bypass another patient’s booking or an authorization restriction.

11. Patient responsibilities

Provide accurate symptoms, relevant medicines, allergies and reports; distinguish information about yourself from information about another person. Upload only material you may lawfully share. Review OCR results before saving or using them and distinguish uploaded documents from clinician-issued records. Follow the treating clinician’s instructions rather than an unverified AI interpretation. Protect downloaded records and consider who can view your screen, email, notifications and shared device.

12. Doctor responsibilities

Doctors must maintain valid registration, provide accurate professional details, practise within their competence and comply with applicable professional, prescribing, confidentiality and telemedicine requirements. Verification or a profile badge is not a guarantee of a clinical outcome. Access patient information only for an authorized purpose. Check AI drafts, drug identity, strength, dose, frequency, timing, duration, tests and follow-up instructions before approving or issuing a prescription. Do not present an unreviewed generated draft as a finalized clinical decision.
